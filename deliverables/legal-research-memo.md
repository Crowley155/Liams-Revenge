## 3. K.S.A. 75-6101 *et seq.* — Kansas Tort Claims Act; Recreational Use–Type Immunity

| Field | Content |
|--------|---------|
| **Citation** | K.S.A. 75-6101–75-6115 (KTCA article); immunity language at **K.S.A. 75-6104(a)(15)** |
| **Claim tested** | (A) Locate recreational-use immunity; (B) statute **expressly excludes** fee-based custodial childcare from that immunity. |
| **Quoted text** (75-6104(a)(15)) | “**(15)** any claim for injuries resulting from the use of any public property **intended or permitted to be used as a park, playground or open area for recreational purposes**, unless: **(A)** The governmental entity or an employee thereof is guilty of **gross and wanton negligence** proximately causing such injury; or **(B)** an employee of the governmental entity commits childhood sexual abuse as defined in K.S.A. 60-523, and amendments thereto;” |
| **Source URL** | https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch75/075_061_0004.html |
| **Application** | **Immunity:** The exception turns on whether the property is **“intended or permitted”** for park/playground/**open recreational** use — **not** on whether a program charges fees. Kansas courts have applied this exception in varied settings (including some **school indoor** facilities — see annotations under 75-6104 on the Revisor page). **The skill file’s claim that fee-based custodial childcare is categorically outside (a)(15) is *not* stated in the statute**; it is a **litigation argument** (primary-purpose / permitted-use fact patterns, gross/wanton negligence, etc.). |
| **Verification status** | **PARTIALLY VERIFIED** — immunity text **verified**; **no statutory “childcare / tuition / custodial program” exclusion** found in K.S.A. 75-6104(a)(15). |

### 4(b) Definitions — K.S.A. 45-217 (including “public agency”)

**Quoted text (excerpts):**  
“(b) ‘Business day’ means any day other than a Saturday, Sunday or day designated as a holiday by the congress of the United States, by the legislature or governor of this state or by the respective political subdivision of this state.”  
“(k) (1) ‘Public agency’ means the state or any political or taxing subdivision of the state or any office, agency or instrumentality thereof, or any other entity receiving or expending and supported in whole or in part by the public funds appropriated by the state or by public funds of any political or taxing subdivision of the state.”  
“(l) (1) ‘Public record’ means any recorded information, regardless of form, characteristics or location, that is made, maintained or kept by or is in the possession of: (A) Any public agency; or (B) any officer or employee of a public agency pursuant to the officer's or employee's official duties and that is related to the functions, activities, programs or operations of any public agency.”

A **county park and recreation district** is also commonly analyzed as a **subdivision or instrumentality** supported by public funds — **but specific entity status can turn on charter, funding, and corporate form** → **VERIFY WITH COUNSEL** for JCPRD’s precise classification in any given record dispute.

**Verification status:** **VERIFIED** (definitions as quoted).

### 4(c) Response timeline — K.S.A. 45-218(d)

**Quoted text:** “Each request for access to a public record shall be acted upon as soon as possible, **but not later than the end of the third business day following the date that the request is received**. If access to the public record is not granted immediately, the custodian shall give a detailed explanation of the cause for further delay and the place and earliest time and date that the record will be available for inspection. If the request for access is denied, the custodian shall provide, upon request, a written statement of the grounds for denial. Such statement shall cite the specific provision of law under which access is denied and shall be furnished to the requester not later than the end of the third business day following the date that the request for the statement is received.”  

**Source URL:** https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch45/045_002_0018.html  

**Verification status:** **VERIFIED**

## 7. 34 C.F.R. Part 104 — Section 504 Regulations (selected sections)

**Source URL (bulk XML used for verbatim pull):** https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2025-01-01/title-34.xml?part=104  

### 7(a) § 104.32 — Location and notification

**Quoted text:** “A recipient that operates a public elementary or secondary education program or activity shall annually: (a) Undertake to identify and locate every qualified handicapped person residing in the recipient's jurisdiction who is not receiving a public education; and (b) Take appropriate steps to notify handicapped persons and their parents or guardians of the recipient's duty under this subpart.”  

**Verification status:** **VERIFIED**

### 7(b) § 104.35 — Evaluation and placement

**Quoted text** (note: eCFR contains apparent OCR typo “belived” for “believed”):  
“(a) *Preplacement evaluation.* A recipient that operates a public elementary or secondary education program or activity shall conduct an evaluation in accordance with the requirements of paragraph (b) of this section of any person who, because of handicap, needs or is belived to need special education or related services **before taking any action with respect to the initial placement** of the person in regular or special education and any subsequent significant change in placement.”  

**Verification status:** **VERIFIED** (text as published in eCFR extract).

### 7(c) § 104.36 — Procedural safeguards

**Quoted text:** “A recipient that operates a public elementary or secondary education program or activity shall establish and implement, with respect to actions regarding the identification, evaluation, or educational placement of persons who, because of handicap, need or are believed to need special instruction or related services, a system of procedural safeguards that includes **notice**, an opportunity for the parents or guardian of the person to examine relevant records, an **impartial hearing** with opportunity for participation by the person's parents or guardian and representation by counsel, and a review procedure. Compliance with the procedural safeguards of section 615 of the Education of the Handicapped Act is one means of meeting this requirement.”  

**Verification status:** **VERIFIED**

### 7(d) § 104.37(b) — Counseling (steering / “bait-and-switch” angle)

**Quoted text:** “A recipient to which this subpart applies that provides personal, academic, or vocational counseling, guidance, or placement services to its students shall provide these services **without discrimination on the basis of handicap**. The recipient shall ensure that qualified handicapped students are **not counseled toward more restrictive career objectives** than are nonhandicapped students with similar interests and abilities.”  

**Verification status:** **VERIFIED**

**Note on skill claim (“written parent request”):** The **Part 104** provisions above establish **evaluation-before-placement** and **procedural safeguards**; they do **not** use the phrase “parent written request.” **OCR’s written guidance / resolution letters** often operationalize how complaints about failure to evaluate are investigated — **VERIFY WITH COUNSEL** and cite specific OCR letters retrieved for filing.

## 8. ADA Amendments Act of 2008 (Pub. L. 110-325) — Disability Definition (42 U.S.C. § 12102)

**Codification note:** ADAAA amendments appear in **42 U.S.C. § 12102** (Title 42), cross-applied in multiple contexts including Section 504/TITLE II analyses.

**Quoted text** (excerpts from 42 U.S.C. § 12102(4)) —  
“(A) The definition of disability in this chapter shall be construed in favor of broad coverage of individuals under this chapter, to the maximum extent permitted by the terms of this chapter.”  
“(B) The term ‘substantially limits’ shall be interpreted consistently with the findings and purposes of the ADA Amendments Act of 2008.”  
“(D) An impairment that is episodic or in remission is a disability if it would substantially limit a major life activity when active.”  
“(E)(i) The determination of whether an impairment substantially limits a major life activity shall be made without regard to the ameliorative effects of mitigating measures such as— (I) medication, medical supplies, equipment, or appliances, … (II) use of assistive technology; (III) reasonable accommodations or auxiliary aids or services; or (IV) learned behavioral or adaptive neurological modifications;”  

**Source URL:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/12102

## Major Deviations from Skill File Framing

1. **KTCA / childcare:** K.S.A. 75-6104(a)(15) **does not** carve out “fee-based custodial childcare.” Immunity depends on whether property is **intended or permitted** as **park/playground/open recreational** area, with **gross and wanton negligence** still a path to liability. Kansas case law applying this provision is **fact-intensive** (see Revisor annotations).  
2. ***Nero* “non-delegable duty”:** Verified secondary materials emphasize **landlord–tenant duty** and **foreseeability**, not necessarily an **independent “non-delegable duty to lessee’s invitees”** rule as broadly stated in the skill file.  
3. ***Beshears*:** **Primary opinion text not retrieved**; treat skill file bullet as **unverified** until counsel pulls the reporter version.  
4. **Section 504 “written parent request”:** **Not spelled out in 29 U.S.C. § 794**; build that argument from **34 C.F.R. Part 104** + **OCR guidance / complaint resolutions** tied to specific facts.
