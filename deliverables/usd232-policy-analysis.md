## Executive Summary

USD 232 maintains a comprehensive policy framework that covers student safety, bullying prevention, incident documentation, and facility use. **None of these policies contain a time-of-day exclusion.** The anti-bullying policy and K.S.A. 72-6147 compliance plan use location-based language — "on school property" — that encompasses JCPRD's OST program operating on Mize Elementary grounds.

Under Lease Section 8(d), JCPRD is required to "abide by… all rules, regulations, and policies adopted by the Board of Education." The policies documented below are the specific Board policies JCPRD was contractually obligated to follow — and the evidence shows it did not.

**Key finding:** A June 2024 board memo from Brian Schwanz (Chief of Operations) describes JCPRD's programs as "invaluable" "before and after school care" and asks the Board to approve the rental contract on the consent agenda. This characterization — childcare, not recreation — directly contradicts the "separate entity" / "independent operator" narrative used by USD 232 officials after the April 2, 2026 incident.

## 1. Anti-Bullying / Harassment Prevention (Policy JDDC)

**Policy code:** JDDC  
**Source:** [USD 232 Bullying & Harassment Prevention](https://www.usd232.org/family-resources/bullying-harassment-prevention)  
**Statutory basis:** K.S.A. 72-6147

### Full Policy Text (from USD 232 website)

> State and federal law and board policy expressly prohibit bullying in any form, including electronic means (cyberbullying) and harassment at school, on school property, and at all school-sponsored activities, programs, or events.
>
> Harassment or bullying in any gesture or written, verbal, graphic, or physical act (including electronically transmitted acts — i.e. internet, cell phone, personal digital assistant, or wireless handheld device) that is reasonably perceived as being motivated either by any actual act or perceived characteristic, such as race, color, religion, ancestry, national origin, gender, sexual orientation, gender identity, and expression; or a mental, physical, or sensory disability or impairment; or by any other distinguishing characteristic. **Such behavior is considered harassment or bullying whether it takes place on school property, at any school-sponsored function, or in a school vehicle.**
>
> If the bullying or harassment is severe, persistent, or pervasive that it creates an intimidating or threatening educational environment; or has the effect of substantially disrupting the orderly operation of the school, school officials may follow school policies in handling the bullying harassment violation.

### Relevance to Liam Crowley Matter

**Critical language: "on school property" — no time-of-day exclusion.**

The JCPRD OST program operates on Mize Elementary grounds — USD 232 school property. The policy covers bullying "whether it takes place on school property, at any school-sponsored function, or in a school vehicle." There is no carve-out for after-hours programs, third-party lessees, or non-district-operated activities.

Under Lease §8(d), JCPRD is required to comply with all Board policies. This policy is one of those Board policies. JCPRD was therefore contractually obligated to address bullying and harassment on school property during its program — and the evidence shows it treated the April 2 incident as "handled" (DOC-009) without a board-policy-compliant investigation.

**Contradiction with institutional response:**  
- Gerri Balthazor (DOC-004): "JCPRD is a separate entity" — implies policy doesn't apply after hours
- Alvie Cater (DOC-012): "JCPRD operates its program independently" — implies JCPRD sets its own rules
- **This policy:** Covers "school property" without exception

## 2. Bullying Prevention Plan (per K.S.A. 72-6147)

**Source:** [USD 232 Bullying Prevention Plan](https://www.usd232.org/family-resources/bullying-harassment-prevention/bullying-prevention-plan)  
**Statutory basis:** K.S.A. 72-6147(c)

### Plan Components (from USD 232 website)

1. **Definition:** Clear definition of bullying, including cyberbullying, shared with staff and students in age-appropriate language. Included in Student Code of Conduct.
2. **Staff training:** Training as appropriate to position on Bullying Prevention initiative.
3. **Student education/training:** Ongoing instruction and support at all grade levels.
4. **Communication:** Utilize district communications (handbooks, website) to inform parents and patrons and encourage support.
5. **Reporting process:** Formal and informal procedures, including anonymous reports.
6. **Investigation process:** **"There is a legal and ethical duty to investigate."**
   - Determine investigation team based on specific circumstances
   - Interview the target (victim), the bully, and bystanders
7. **Consequences:** Applied consistently across the district based on frequency, severity, and duration.
8. **Chronic offenders:**
   - Refer to school support staff (counselor, social worker)
   - Refer for outside mental health assistance
   - **Report to law enforcement if a crime has been committed**
9. **Continuously evaluate** effectiveness and revise as appropriate.

### Relevance to Liam Crowley Matter

**"There is a legal and ethical duty to investigate."** — This is the district's own published standard.

Under §8(d), JCPRD must follow this plan. The evidence shows:

| Plan requirement | What happened | Evidence |
|-----------------|---------------|----------|
| Investigation duty | JCPRD said incident was "handled" — no documented investigation | DOC-009 |
| Interview victim, bully, bystanders | Parent alleges same-room questioning with older child; no separation protocol | DOC-016 |
| Consistent consequences | No documented consequences for aggressor; JCPRD "won't remove older student" | DOC-003 |
| Report to law enforcement if crime committed | Battery is a crime; no evidence JCPRD reported to law enforcement (parent filed own report) | DOC-010, DOC-001 |
| Staff training | Leigh White references training (DOC-009) but training did not prevent five-staff/zero-witness outcome | DOC-003, DOC-008 |

## 3. Student Discipline — Suspension & Expulsion (Policy JDD / K.S.A. 72-6114)

**Policy code:** JDD  
**Source:** USD 232 Board Policy Manual, Section J (Students)  
**Statutory basis:** K.S.A. 72-6114

### Statutory Text (K.S.A. 72-6114 — VERIFIED PRIMARY)

> The board of education of any school district may suspend or expel... any pupil guilty of any of the following:
>
> **(a)** Willful violation of any published regulation for student conduct adopted or approved by the board of education;
>
> **(c)** Conduct which endangers the safety of others or which substantially impinges upon or invades the rights of others **at school, on school property, or at a school supervised activity**;
>
> **(e)** Conduct **at school, on school property, or at a school supervised activity** which, if the pupil is an adult, constitutes the commission of a misdemeanor...

### Relevance to Liam Crowley Matter

**Location-based language without time-of-day limitation.** The statute uses "at school, on school property, or at a school supervised activity" — not "during school hours." A physical battery on school property during an after-school program falls squarely within subsections (c) and (e).

The district's position that it "lacks authority after 3:45" (DOC-006, as reported by parent) is inconsistent with this statute's plain language.

## 4. Community Use of School Facilities (Policy KF)

**Source:** [USD 232 Facility Rental Information](https://www.usd232.org/departments/facilities/facility-rental-information)

### Policy Summary

- School activities have **first priority** for facility use
- **The Board of Education, through the Superintendent or their designee, reserves the right to approve or reject any request** for use of district facilities
- Community organizations may use facilities "at reasonable times and rates"
- Priority system: (1) school-related instructional programs, (2) district-sponsored activities, (3) district youth activities, (4) other community groups

### Relevance to Liam Crowley Matter

**The Board controls facility access.** JCPRD's presence at Mize Elementary exists because the Board approved it. The Board has the power to approve or reject — and therefore has ongoing authority over and awareness of programs operating on its property.

This undermines the "separate entity" narrative: you cannot claim no responsibility for a program you approved, housed, and characterized as "invaluable to our families."

## 5. Emergency Safety Interventions (Policy GAAF)

**Source:** [USD 232 Emergency Safety Interventions](https://www.usd232.org/family-resources/emergency-safety-interventions)

### Key Requirements

- **Same-day parent notification** required
- Written documentation to parents **no later than the school day following the incident**, including:
  - Events leading up to the incident
  - Student behaviors that necessitated the intervention
  - Steps taken to transition the student back
  - Date, time, type of intervention, duration, and **personnel involved**
  - Space for **parent feedback or comments**
  - Statement inviting parents to **schedule a meeting** to discuss incident and prevent future incidents
  - School contact information

### Relevance to Liam Crowley Matter

While GAAF specifically addresses seclusion/restraint interventions, the documentation and notification standards it establishes represent the Board's expectations for incident response. Under §8(d), JCPRD should align with these standards.

The JCPRD incident report (DOC-020):
- Was same-day (DOC-017) — meets timing
- But parent disputes accuracy (DOC-016)
- Does not include space for parent feedback
- Does not invite parent to meeting as part of the documentation
- Parent characterizes it as rushed, biased, and incomplete

## 7. Safety & Security Program

**Source:** [USD 232 Safety & Security](https://www.usd232.org/departments/safety-and-security)

### Summary

USD 232 maintains a comprehensive safety program with three categories:
- **Communication:** IP mass notification, paging, intercom systems; updated handheld radios
- **Secure Facilities:** Security cameras, controlled entry, ADA compliance, modernized door access
- **Response to Crisis:** Lockdown, lockout, shelter-in-place, hold-in-place, evacuation protocols

### Relevance to Liam Crowley Matter

The district invests significantly in physical security infrastructure on school property. Security cameras, controlled entry, and communication systems cover the same grounds where JCPRD operates. The district's safety commitment does not include a temporal limitation or a carve-out for after-hours programs.

The dissonance: the district maintains physical security infrastructure 24/7 on property where it claims no safety responsibility after 3:45 PM.

## 8. KEY EVIDENCE: The Schwanz Board Memo (June 26, 2024)

**Source:** [USD 232 Board Document — JCPRD Rental Fees Alert](https://usd232.diligent.community/document/4c7ee92c-6017-426b-8fea-c87c2e2dc16f/)  
**Author:** Brian Schwanz, Chief of Operations  
**To:** USD 232 Board of Education; Dr. Cory Gibson, Superintendent  
**Date:** June 26, 2024

### Full Memo Text

> During the Board of Education's regularly scheduled meeting on August 5, 2024, the Board of Education will be asked to approve the rental contract agreement with Johnson County Parks and Recreation District (JCPRD). The contract with JCPRD will be presented on the Consent Agenda.
>
> We reviewed the fees JCPRD is paying to the district for its elementary before and after school care as well as its summer program. As you can imagine, **the programs JCPRD provides are invaluable to some of our families that need childcare during the summer and before and after school**. Currently, the rental fee is $8 per hour per space at the schools that house JCPRD in the summer as well as before and after school care. JCPRD typically uses two spaces at each school when these services are provided. It has been this rate for the past four years when it was raised from $7 to $8 per hour per space. At this time, we are asking the Board to keep the current rates for the 2024-2025 school year. We have alerted JCPRD that we will be reviewing our hourly rates and would notify them by February 2025 if we have a small raise for the 2025-2026 school year.

### Why This Memo Matters

This document destroys three institutional narratives simultaneously:

| Narrative | Memo says | Contradiction |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| **"JCPRD is a separate entity"** | Board directly approves JCPRD contract on consent agenda; Chief of Operations manages relationship | The Board controls whether JCPRD operates on its property |
| **"JCPRD operates independently"** | Programs described as "invaluable to **our** families" | USD 232 treats JCPRD as integrated service for **district** families |
| **"This is a recreational program"** | Described as "elementary before and after school **care**" / "**childcare**" | USD 232's own characterization is childcare, not recreation |

**Additional significance:**
- **"Two spaces at each school"** — JCPRD uses multiple rooms, not just the playground
- **Board-level awareness:** The Board and Superintendent receive this memo; they know JCPRD is operating on their property serving their families
- **Financial relationship:** USD 232 receives $8/hour/space — there is a revenue relationship, not just a neutral lease
- **Four-year relationship:** Rate has been $8 since 2020-21; this is a long-standing, Board-approved arrangement

## 10. How This Connects to Legal Strategy

### Contract Track (Lease §8(d) Breach)
These policies are the **specific obligations** JCPRD was required to follow under §8(d). The anti-bullying policy (JDDC) and the Bullying Prevention Plan are the most directly violated. The Schwanz memo proves Board-level control and awareness.

### Tort Track (Gross/Wanton Negligence)
The policies define the **standard of care** that a reasonable entity operating on school property would follow. Departure from these known standards — particularly the "legal and ethical duty to investigate" — supports the argument that JCPRD's conduct went beyond ordinary negligence.

### Regulatory Track (KDHE/KORA)
These policies, combined with K.A.R. childcare licensing standards, create overlapping obligations. KORA requests to both USD 232 and JCPRD should seek full policy compliance documentation.
