## 2. The Central Legal Question: Gross and Wanton Negligence

### 2.1 Statutory gateway

**K.S.A. 75-6104(a)(15)** bars claims for injuries from use of public property **intended or permitted** as a **park, playground, or open area** for **recreational purposes**, **unless** the governmental entity or employee is guilty of **gross and wanton negligence** proximately causing the injury (**VERIFIED PRIMARY** — Kansas Revisor, per `legal-research-memo.md`).

### 2.2 Kansas gloss on gross and wanton (verification mixed)

Research summarized the standard as requiring conduct **beyond ordinary negligence**: **realization of imminent danger** plus **reckless disregard** or **indifference** to **probable consequences**. Cases commonly cited in Kansas discussions include *Willard v. City of Kansas City*, 235 Kan. 655 (1984); *Lee v. City of Fort Scott*, 238 Kan. 421 (1985); *Bonewell v. City of Derby*, 236 Kan. 589 (1985); *Hopkins v. State*, 237 Kan. 601 (1985); *Gruhin v. City of Overland Park*, 17 Kan. App. 2d 388 (1992) (**VERIFY WITH COUNSEL** — pull full opinions; **not** verified from primary text in this workspace).

**Sufficiency vs. insufficiency (high level):** Courts have granted summary judgment where proof of gross/wanton was **missing** (*Willard*, *Lee*—**VERIFY WITH COUNSEL**). **Gross/wanton can be a fact issue** precluding summary judgment when evidence creates a genuine dispute (*Gruhin*—**VERIFY WITH COUNSEL**). In **public park**-type contexts, **gross/wanton** is often emphasized as the path past immunity (*Bonewell*, *Hopkins*—**VERIFY WITH COUNSEL**).

### 2.3 Mapping our facts (honest)

**Facts favoring gross/wanton (arguable):**

| Theme | Fact pattern (documented) | Limitation |
|--------|---------------------------|------------|
| **Notice** | Prior concerns about older students / group dynamics raised to school and JCPRD context (DOC-003, DOC-006, DOC-008, DOC-023) | Notice to **USD 232** may be **stronger** than to JCPRD depending on **who knew what, when**—**discovery** |

| **Post-incident conduct** | Coercive **same-room** questioning; disputed **accident** vs. **intentional** framing; “no medical treatment necessary” vs. pediatric escalation (DOC-016, C-1–C-3) | May bear more on **misrepresentation/reckless institutional conduct** than **playground breach**—**theory-dependent** |
| **Misaligned reporting** | Incident report witness list vs. “end of conflict” only (C-1) | Supports **credibility** and **punitive-ish narrative**; **immunity analysis** still centers on **conduct causing physical injury** |

**Facts cutting against gross/wanton (defense narrative):**

- **Novel, severe conduct** not yet shown on **prior similar assaults** by the same aggressor in OST (unless discovery yields **pattern**).
- **Child-on-child** violence can be framed as **unpredictable** absent **specific** threat knowledge—*Beshears*, *Sly*, *Glaser* line (**see §4**).
- **Immunity cases** teach that **lack of supervision** does **not** automatically **strip** (a)(15) (*Jackson*, *Nichols*).

**Honest conclusion:** As of **April 10, 2026**, the **public record** supports **strong ordinary-negligence themes** and **regulatory breach** narratives. Whether that crosses into **gross and wanton** is **unsettled** and **fact- and discovery-dependent**. **Counsel should assume** defendants will move for **summary judgment** on immunity and **failure to meet gross/wanton**.

### 3.7 Breach of contract — Lease Section 8(d) (vs. JCPRD; USD 232 enforcement failure)

**THIS CLAIM BYPASSES KTCA RECREATIONAL IMMUNITY ENTIRELY.**

The KTCA governs **tort** claims — "negligent or wrongful act or omission" (K.S.A. 75-6103). Breach of contract is a **categorically separate** cause of action. K.S.A. 75-6104(a)(15) recreational immunity applies to "claims for injuries resulting from the use of public property" — tort claims within the KTCA framework. A contract claim is **outside** that framework. Kansas law holds that government entities have "the same responsibilities and liabilities as a private entity" in business transactions (*KPERS v. **The JCPRD Handbook — Smoking Gun (new evidence):**

JCPRD's Olathe Program Handbook ([jcprd.com/2075](https://www.jcprd.com/2075/Olathe-Program-Handbook)), Policy 1 (Program Administration), states:

This is a **published, institutional admission** that JCPRD operates independently from board policies. It directly contradicts Section 8(d) of the lease, which **requires** compliance. JCPRD's handbook reveals that non-compliance was not an oversight but a **deliberate operational posture**. Contradiction C-5 in the evidence catalog already documents Alvie Cater's matching rhetoric: "JCPRD leases space from the district and operates its program independently" (DOC-012).

**Standing: Third-Party Beneficiary Analysis**

Under *Kincaid v. Dess*, No. 107,970 (Kan. Ct. App. 2013) — **VERIFIED PRIMARY**:

- Third-party beneficiaries must be **intended** (not incidental) — only intended beneficiaries have standing (*State ex rel. Stovall v. Reliance Ins. Co.*, 278 Kan. 777, 793 (2005) — **VERIFIED SECONDARY** via *Kincaid*).
- The test is **contract interpretation** applying general construction rules: whole agreement, plain meaning, intent from the four corners (*Kincaid*; *Fasse v. Lower Heating*, 241 Kan. 387 (1987) — **VERIFIED SECONDARY**).
- Beneficiary **need not be personally named** — membership in a **designated class** or **identifiable as a benefitted person** suffices (*Hartford Fire Ins. Co. v. Western Fire Ins. Co.*, 226 Kan. 197, 210 (1979) — **VERIFIED SECONDARY** via *Kincaid*).
- **Burden** is on the party asserting standing (*Byers v. Snyder*, 44 Kan. 2d 380 (2010) — **VERIFIED SECONDARY** via *Kincaid*).
- Key inquiry: "**whether the [plaintiffs] were intended to be benefited by the contract provision in question**" (*Kincaid*).

**Arguments for intended-beneficiary status:**
- §8(d) requires compliance with **anti-bullying policies** (K.S.A. 72-6147) and **discipline rules** (K.S.A. 72-6114) — statutes whose **sole purpose** is protecting students.
- Students are an **identifiable class** of persons for whose benefit these policy-compliance provisions operate.
- JCPRD's own Handbook Policy 15 acknowledges custodial duty: "JCPRD strives to maintain a safe, healthy, and secure environment for children and youth **under JCPRD's care and supervision.**"
- A provision requiring a lessee to follow child safety policies on school property has **no plausible purpose** other than protecting the children in those programs.
- The lease governs an **on-site childcare program serving enrolled students** — the connection between §8(d) compliance and student safety is **direct**, not **incidental**.

**Adversarial analysis (3 layers):**

1. **Defense:** Lease runs **district–JCPRD** only; students are **incidental** beneficiaries of an **administrative compliance** clause.
   **Plaintiff:** §8(d) mandates compliance with statutes whose **exclusive purpose** is student protection (72-6147, 72-6114); students are the **identifiable class** of intended beneficiaries; *Kincaid* — need not be named.
   **Defense reply:** §8(d) is **boilerplate** lease compliance language; the district's **interest** is **operational control and risk allocation**, not creating **enforceable rights** for individual students. *Stovall* — incidental benefit insufficient.

2. **Defense:** Even if TPB standing exists, **contract damages** do not include **personal injury** — plaintiff must go through **tort** (and face KTCA).
   **Plaintiff:** Kansas UCC K.S.A. 84-2-715 recognizes personal injury as consequential damages from breach; contract damages include **foreseeable consequences** of breach; a child's physical injury from a supervision failure that constitutes breach of a safety obligation is **directly foreseeable**.
   **Defense reply:** UCC applies to **goods**, not **service/lease** contracts; non-UCC personal injury via contract is **uncertain** under Kansas law (**VERIFY WITH COUNSEL**).

3. **Defense:** JCPRD's handbook characterization is **not** an admission of breach — it describes the **school district's expectation**, not JCPRD's contractual obligation. JCPRD may have **operationally** complied with specific board policies even if the handbook uses "independent" language.
   **Plaintiff:** The handbook is JCPRD's **own published policy statement** to parents; "independently in both policy and procedure" is unambiguous; factual record shows **specific** non-compliance (no board-policy-compliant investigation, no safety plan, disputed incident report — DOC-003, DOC-013, DOC-016, C-1–C-10).
   **Defense reply:** Individual incident ≠ systemic breach; handbook is **aspirational**, not evidence of **contract** default.

**Strength rating:** **MODERATE to STRONG** on **breach** elements (contract exists, §8(d) is clear, JCPRD's handbook is damning, specific factual breaches documented). **MODERATE** on **standing** (strong argument but **no on-all-fours Kansas case** for school lease TPB — **VERIFY WITH COUNSEL**). **UNCERTAIN** on **damages type** (personal injury through contract vs. economic only — **VERIFY WITH COUNSEL**).

**Strategic significance:** Even if standing is uncertain, the **contract theory changes the negotiation dynamic**. Defendants cannot rely on recreational immunity as a summary judgment shield if a parallel contract claim survives. The **threat** of a contract claim that bypasses KTCA may be as valuable as the claim itself.

## 4. The Recreational Immunity Problem

### 4.1 Statutory text (verified)

**K.S.A. 75-6104(a)(15)** — immunity for claims from use of property **intended or permitted** as **park, playground, or open area** for **recreational purposes**, **unless** **gross and wanton negligence** or **sexual abuse** exception applies (**VERIFIED PRIMARY** — Revisor).

**Critical verified correction:** The **statute does not** exclude **fee-based** or **licensed childcare** by **program type** (`legal-research-memo.md`).

### 4.5 Educational-purpose argument — Ozuk/Jackson "more than incidental" reframe (NEW)

**Legal basis:** *Jackson v. USD 259*, 268 Kan. 319 (2000) — **VERIFIED PRIMARY** — adopted *Ozuk v. River Grove Board of Education*, 281 Ill. App. 3d 239 (1996), which held:

> "compulsory physical education and recreation have different aims: whereas the former seeks to instruct, the latter aspires merely to amuse."

Jackson, Syll. ¶ 8: "if a school gymnasium is encouraged, intended, or permitted to be used for recess, extracurricular events, or other recreational, noncompulsory activities, then K.S.A. 75-6104(o) would apply, **provided that the recreational use was more than incidental.**"

The court **reversed and remanded** for a factual determination. This means the question of whether property is "intended or permitted for recreational purposes" is a **fact question** that can survive summary judgment when evidence cuts both ways.

**JCPRD's own characterization of OST (from JCPRD website and Olathe Program Handbook — verified April 2026):**

| Source | Quote | Character |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| Vision statement | "exceptional child care, **educational opportunities**, and enriching programs" | Educational |
| Mission statement | "prioritize **well-being, character building, and education** to inspire a **love of learning and curiosity**" | Educational |
| Philosophy | "enriching activities that stimulate **creativity, critical thinking, and exploration**" | Educational |
| Program goals | "opportunities to **extend school day learning experiences**" | Educational |
| Policy 16 | "Student involvement in community service is a **core curriculum objective**" | Educational |
| Policy 27 | "before- and after-school **enrichment opportunities** in a group care setting" | Educational |
| Policy 19 (Dress) | "**A portion** of the day will be spent outdoors" | Outdoor = minority of time |
| OST page | "enrichment opportunities focused on creativity, environmental literacy, fitness, health, innovation, and **STEM**" | Educational |
| OST page | "social and emotional learning, helping children develop problem-solving, self-confidence, teamwork, mentoring, and goal-setting skills" | Developmental |
| Program activities | "science experiments, cooking, health, fitness, environmental education, and recreation" | Recreation is **one item** in an educational list |

**The argument:** OST **"seeks to instruct"** — STEM, environmental education, social-emotional learning, homework support, community service. JCPRD does **not** describe OST as a recreational program. Outdoor playground time is described as **"a portion"** of the day — one component of a multi-activity educational enrichment program. Under Ozuk/Jackson, if the recreational use of the property during OST hours was **incidental** to the program's primary educational purpose, the "intended or permitted for recreational purposes" threshold creates a **genuine fact question** precluding summary judgment.

**Location-specific analysis (critical — from research):**

| Location | Immunity likelihood | Authority |
|----------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Playground (outdoor)** | **HIGH** — a school playground is almost certainly "intended or permitted" for recreation regardless of program running on it | *Lanning*, *Nichols*, *Jackson* property-character rule |
| **Cafeteria (during homework/STEM)** | **LOW to MODERATE** — Jackson explicitly disclaimed extending "open area" to libraries, lecture halls, cafeterias. *Abrams v. Oak Lawn-Hometown* (Ill. 2014) held a "cafetorium" used for educational purposes was NOT intended for recreation even during after-school event. *Poston* distinguished "pure school lunch room" from integral-to-gym commons. | *Jackson* (dicta); *Abrams* (**VERIFIED PRIMARY** — Illinois, persuasive); *Poston* (distinction) |
| **Gymnasium (during structured activity)** | **FACT-DEPENDENT** — Jackson remanded on this exact question. If gym used for OST educational activities (not free-play), Ozuk's instruction/amusement distinction applies. | *Jackson*/*Ozuk* (remand); *Barrett* (football field = immune) |
| **Transition areas / hallways** | **MODERATE** — *Poston*/*Robison* extend immunity to areas "integral" to recreational use; instructional hallway not analyzed | *Poston* (commons as transitional) |

**Persuasive authority — *Abrams v. Oak Lawn-Hometown Middle School*, 2014 IL App (1st) 132987** — **VERIFIED PRIMARY** (Justia):
- **Facts:** Student injured in "Cafetorium" during after-school National Junior Honor Society event (~6:40 p.m.).
- **Holding:** "There is no indication in the record that the school has ever intended or permitted the Cafetorium to be used for recreational purposes." Uses described were "**all educational or incidental to educational uses, which are nonrecreational uses**" (daily lunch, assemblies, clubs, ceremonies). Band/chorus/drama: "**part of the educational process and are not recreational uses**" — cites *Ozuk*.
- **Application:** Directly persuasive for arguing that a cafeteria used during OST for homework, STEM, meals, and structured enrichment is **not** "intended or permitted for recreational purposes."
- **Limitation:** Illinois statute explicitly includes "buildings or other enclosed recreational facilities"; Kansas wording differs. Persuasive only.

**Limitations (honest):**
- *Lane v. Atchison* (2007) — **VERIFIED PRIMARY** (via *Poston* quotations) — rejects equating "more than incidental" with "primary purpose." **Educational purpose is not automatically dispositive** — the court still looks at whether recreation was "intended or permitted" and "more than incidental."
- Defense will argue the playground was **permitted** for recreation **regardless** of what program is using it — property character, not program character.
- **Best case:** creates a **factual question** surviving summary judgment — not a guaranteed win on immunity.
- **Strongest when:** assault occurred **inside** (cafeteria during homework, gym during structured activity) rather than on the playground.

**STOP:** Determine the **exact location** of the assault. If it occurred in the **cafeteria** or **gym** during a structured educational activity, the Ozuk/Jackson/Abrams argument is **significantly** stronger than if it occurred on the **playground** during free outdoor time.
