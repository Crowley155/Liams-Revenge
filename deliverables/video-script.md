# Video Script: "Why You Should Care"

**Format:** AI-generated talking head (HeyGen recommended)
**Tone:** Candid, raw, controlled anger — not self-pitying. Speaking directly to other parents.
**Runtime:** ~2.5 minutes
**Hosting:** Vimeo or YouTube (unlisted), embedded in the Case Command Center

---

My son was assaulted at school. He's six.

A nine-year-old attacked him on the playground at an after-school program run by Johnson County Parks and Recreation inside his elementary school. Five staff members were outside. Not one of them saw it happen.

He spent a week at home on his pediatrician's orders. His doctor told us that if the spinal inflammation had been any worse, we'd be talking about emergency surgery at a trauma center. Instead, the program called it an accident, wrote a report that contradicts the medical evidence, and told us they'd already "handled it."

When we went to the school, they told us it wasn't their problem. JCPRD is a "separate entity," they said. When we went to the district, they said the same thing and closed the door.

Here's what they don't tell you: the district's own lease requires that program to follow every single school board policy. Anti-bullying. Supervision. Reporting. All of it. The district's own Chief of Operations wrote a memo to the school board calling the program "invaluable childcare for our families." Their own website markets it as a district service.

But when a child gets hurt? Suddenly it's not theirs.

My son probably won't see justice from this. The child who did this won't face real consequences. The staff who failed to supervise won't be held accountable. The district officials who deflected and obstructed won't lose a minute of sleep.

And I've made peace with that.

What I can't make peace with is the idea that the next parent walks into this blind. That they trust the marketing. That they believe "school property" means "school standards." That when something goes wrong, they hit the same wall of bureaucratic double-speak we did — and they don't have the time, the resources, or the sheer stubbornness to push back.

So my son asked me to do something about it. He's six, and his instinct was to help other kids. That's more moral clarity than every administrator we've dealt with combined.

This site exists because of him. Everything here — every statute, every email, every policy, every contradiction — is documented, sourced, and public. Not because we want sympathy. Because we want you to have what we didn't: information.

Here's what I need from you.

Show up at school board meetings. Not just when your kid gets hurt — before that. Ask questions. Ask who's actually supervising your children. Ask what policies apply and who enforces them. Ask what happens when something goes wrong. And when they give you corporate non-answers, don't accept it.

File complaints. Not because it will change anything overnight, but because complaints create records. Records create patterns. Patterns create accountability. Every parent who files a complaint with KDHE, with DCF, with the county licensing board, makes it harder for these agencies to look the other way.

Demand transparency. Demand that school districts publish which third-party programs operate in their buildings, what standards they're held to, and what the district's enforcement authority actually is. This shouldn't require a legal research project to figure out.

And finally — if you're a parent without the resources to fight this fight, know that everything we've built here is free, it's public, and it's yours. Use it. Share it. Send it to your attorney. Send it to your school board member. Send it to the local news.

The system won't fix itself. But we can make it very uncomfortable to keep pretending nothing is broken.

Thank you.
